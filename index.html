Theia / Product direction

The schedule says what should happen.
The field decides what can happen next.

Theia is being designed as a decision layer for specialty contractors. It connects plans to current conditions, surfaces executable options, and keeps the responsible leader in control.

Observe the change

Connect work packages to areas, constraints, materials, equipment, and field evidence. Missing information stays “needs confirmation.”

Recommend the next move

Match ready work to eligible crews. Explain qualifications, availability, equipment conflicts, assumptions, and affected commitments.

Learn from the outcome

A leader approves or modifies the decision. Communicate the assignment and compare actual execution with the expected result.

Theia for trades / Initial focus

Built around the crew decision.

Starting with electrical contractors managing multiple projects, crews, areas, and dependencies. Designed for general foremen, superintendents, operations managers, and project managers who make the daily plan executable.

Readiness brings together drawings, materials, access, inspections, and predecessors. Crew matching considers capability, certifications, shift, location, crew size, and equipment—not just availability.

Field scenarios / Illustrative examples

Different constraint.
Same decision loop.

The planned zone is not ready

Mechanical work blocks electrical rough-in. Check alternatives, drawings, staging, access, inspection, and lift availability before seeking approval for a crew move.

Material arrives before the plan catches up

Cable tray arrives early. Reassess nearby work packages and crew capability, then compare pulling work forward with holding the existing plan.

The qualified crew is unavailable

A medium-voltage foreman is absent and a lift is committed elsewhere. Exclude ineligible crews and expose qualifications, equipment conflicts, and mobilization assumptions.

An inspection changes tomorrow’s plan

Link failed inspection and rework to affected packages. Separate blocked work from executable alternatives and observe the actual recovery result.

Competitive positioning / ALICE + Theia

Different operating layer.
Better decisions in the field.

ALICE and Theia overlap on planning, constraints, resources, production rates, resequencing, and recovery scenarios. The distinction is what happens around the schedule when field conditions change.

ALICE

Optimizes modeled scenarios

Publicly described capabilities include generating and comparing schedule scenarios using activities, dependencies, constraints, resources, production rates, materials, and equipment.

Core object
Schedule activity + modeled resources + scheduling constraints

THEIA

Operates the next executable move

Theia is designed to maintain live work-readiness and help trade leaders decide which eligible crew should perform which executable work next.

Core object
Executable work package + live readiness + actual resource capability

The precise difference: it is not simply which constraints each product can represent. It is who discovers, validates, and maintains those constraints as field conditions change. ALICE optimizes the schedule; Theia is designed to help operate the trade.

Theia for GCs / Future direction

Coordinate the handoff.
Respect the trade’s authority.

A future GC view would surface cross-trade readiness, constraints, milestone risks, and shared recovery options. Subcontractors retain control of their crews. Worker details, internal labor costs, and proprietary production data are not shared by default.

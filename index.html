The problem / Near-term coordination

The schedule isn’t the whole story.

Construction teams already plan through schedules, lookaheads, huddles, field walks, calls, texts, emails, daily reports, and direct conversations. But the near-term plan keeps moving.

One trade runs behind. An inspection does not happen. A delivery misses its window. An area is not ready. A crew changes its plan. Theia is intended to keep the people and near-term plans affected by those changes synchronized.

Plans + commitments

Connect what each trade intends to do, where, and when—with the responsible people and current commitments.

Changes + impacts

Surface when a field change affects another trade, area, activity, or upcoming commitment.

One shared picture

Keep the revised near-term plan and the people who need to act on it aligned.

The product / A live coordination layer for the field

Keep the GC and trades synchronized.

Theia connects near-term plans, progress, constraints, changes, and commitments across the field team. It supports coordination without taking decision authority away from the people closest to the work.

GC ↔ Sub

Keep the GC and subcontractors aligned on what is planned, what is ready, what changed, and what happens next.

How Theia works / Plan → reality → response

Six steps around the change.

Theia is a coordination loop for maintaining shared operational awareness as conditions change—not another place to create a master schedule.

  1. Capture plans, commitments, progress, constraints, and changes
  2. Understand what trades intend to do and what is happening
  3. Detect affected people, areas, activities, and commitments
  4. Surface what changed, who is affected, and what matters
  5. Respond with the superintendent’s decision
  6. Synchronize the revised plan and new commitments

Human-in-the-loop / Field judgment stays central

Your field team makes the call.

Theia supports field judgment; it does not replace it. Experienced superintendents hold context software may not have: actual conditions, safety concerns, means and methods, relationships, contractual considerations, and project-specific realities.

Theia’s roleReduce the work required to gather, connect, relay, and maintain operational information—so the superintendent can make an informed decision and keep the team aligned.

Competitive positioning / ALICE + Theia

Different operating layer.
Better decisions in the field.

ALICE and Theia overlap on planning, constraints, resources, production rates, resequencing, and recovery scenarios. The distinction is what happens around the schedule when field conditions change.

ALICE

Optimizes modeled scenarios

Publicly described capabilities include generating and comparing schedule scenarios using activities, dependencies, constraints, resources, production rates, materials, and equipment.

Core object
Schedule activity + modeled resources + scheduling constraints

THEIA

Operates the next executable move

Theia is designed to maintain live work-readiness and help trade leaders decide which eligible crew should perform which executable work next.

Core object
Executable work package + live readiness + actual resource capability

The precise difference: it is not simply which constraints each product can represent. It is who discovers, validates, and maintains those constraints as field conditions change. ALICE optimizes the schedule; Theia is designed to help operate the trade.

Product philosophy / Clear boundaries

Assist the field team.
Don’t replace its judgment.

Theia is not an AI superintendent, a replacement for the superintendent, another CPM or master scheduling tool, or a promise that every field condition is already known.

It does not replace P6 or CPM scheduling.It does not eliminate huddles, field walks, or superintendent judgment.It does not claim to predict every disruption or prove savings before the product exists.
